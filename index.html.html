"Your job is not to make the account look green. Your job is to make the account healthy."

The great SDM

  • Creates operational control instead of reacting to every problem
  • Knows the contract and prevents accidental scope creep
  • Turns metrics into decisions: metric → trend → root cause → action → owner → due date
  • Builds trust without over-promising
  • Knows when to solve vs. when to escalate
  • Understands capacity, skills, attrition, succession
  • Understands financial consequences of operational calls
  • Treats improvement as normal operations, not a side project

The wrong SDM

  • Runs meetings all day, can't explain business impact
  • Reports SLA percentages without root causes
  • Says yes to out-of-scope work to keep the peace
  • Escalates everything upward instead of owning outcomes
  • Knows the team but not the contract
  • Knows revenue but not cost or margin
  • Builds decks that hide operational reality

Your operating philosophy

Click a line off as it becomes second nature.

  1. Know the contract — if you don't know what was sold, you can't manage delivery
  2. Know the service — dependencies, failure points, business impact
  3. Know the people — capacity, skills and succession determine survival under pressure
  4. Know the customer — what matters to them, not what's easy to report
  5. Know the numbers — revenue, cost, utilization, margin, forecast
  6. Protect scope — being helpful is good, giving away funded work is not
  7. Manage trends, not snapshots — a green number today can hide a deteriorating system
  8. Escalate with solutions — never throw a problem over the wall
  9. Build systems — an operation that works without constant personal intervention
  10. Think one level above your title

The metric discipline

Metric → Target → Trend → Root cause → Business impact → Corrective action → Owner → Due date → Expected result
Example: SLA 96.8% vs 98% target; declining for three months; root cause is L2 capacity plus recurring incidents; business impact is delayed resolution; action is capacity rebalance plus problem elimination; owner is service lead; recovery expected next month.

Escalation — bad vs. good

Bad escalation"We have a problem and need help."
Good escalation"Service X has had three P1 incidents in four weeks. The immediate cause is addressed, but the underlying capacity constraint remains. We need two additional specialist resources or approval for a platform change. Without action, another outage is likely."
